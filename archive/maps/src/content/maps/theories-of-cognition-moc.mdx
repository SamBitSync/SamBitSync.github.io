Different theories bring different features of cognition into focus. Some start with representations and operations, others with bodily activity, relations to the environment, or processes unfolding through time. This map offers a first orientation to those emphases.

Its three clusters are a browsing aid. Membership in a cluster does not make two theories equivalent, and placement in different clusters does not by itself make them incompatible.

## Reading the relationships

The diagram names affinities and tensions to investigate in the linked notes. Each label compresses a larger position, so it should raise a question rather than settle the comparison. Which account of representation is involved? Where does the cognitive system end? What would count as explaining its activity?

For a more explicit comparison of explanatory commitments, see [Where theories of cognition disagree](/maps/research-programmes/). That map distinguishes the levels at which two accounts may agree or conflict.
