# Embodied Cognition

## The Idea

The body is not life support for a symbol-processing brain. Cognition is shaped, and on stronger readings partly constituted, by having a particular body acting in a particular environment. The evidence points in one direction from several sides: abstract thought runs on bodily metaphor (warm personality, ideas are up, argument is war), reading "kick" activates leg motor cortex, and people gesture while thinking even when no one can see them, with reasoning suffering if you prevent it.

The versions matter. Weak embodiment says the body constrains an otherwise computational mind. Strong embodiment says some cognitive processes literally are sensorimotor processes. Radical embodiment drops internal representation entirely in favor of direct organism-environment coupling. The disagreements with computationalism and functionalism live at those stronger grades: if the body matters constitutively, then not any implementation will do.

## Why I Keep It

It generates concrete questions for multilingual cognition. Do bilinguals simulate differently in L1 and L2? Languages encode motion differently (manner versus path), so does translating an action verb require re-running a different simulation? Languages with absolute spatial terms come with different navigation strategies, which is embodiment reaching into culture. It is one vertex of the theories-of-cognition space I keep triangulating against.

## Tensions

- Prime numbers, justice, infinity: not everything reduces to sensorimotor experience. Embodiment may be scaffolding rather than constitution.
- Disembodied systems keep succeeding at language tasks. Either they lack understanding in some way that matters, or embodiment is one route among several.
- Simulation effects might follow comprehension rather than constitute it; correlation is not constitution.
