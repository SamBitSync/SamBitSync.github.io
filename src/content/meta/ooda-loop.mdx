# OODA Loop

## The Idea

Boyd's loop for acting in environments that change while you decide: observe what is happening, orient it against your mental models, decide, act. Acting changes the situation, so the loop restarts. The advantage goes to whoever cycles faster: quick learning beats perfect planning.

Boyd's own emphasis was on Orient. Your models, training, and history shape what you notice and how you read it, so bad orientation ruins good data. The loop is only as good as the models it filters through.

## Why I Keep It

Debugging and model development are OODA-shaped: observe the error, orient to likely causes, decide on one test, run it, loop. It complements Pólya. Pólya is for well-defined problems; OODA is for situations where the problem itself keeps shifting.

## Tensions

- Fast iteration can mean shallow understanding. When to slow down?
- The loop privileges action. Sometimes the right move is to observe longer.
- Orient is a black box: nothing in the loop tells you how to improve your models.
