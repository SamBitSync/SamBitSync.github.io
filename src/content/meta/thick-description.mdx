# Thick Description

## The Idea

Geertz, borrowing from Ryle: "the boy rapidly contracted his right eyelid" is thin description. "The boy winked conspiratorially at his friend while the teacher wasn't looking" is thick. Same physical event; the difference is meaning. A twitch, a wink, and a parody of a wink are indistinguishable on camera and completely different acts. You cannot tell them apart by watching harder. You have to interpret the symbolic system they happen inside.

The claim: adequate social explanation interprets meaning rather than just recording behavior. Actions make sense within shared codes, local norms, and the agent's own understanding of what they are doing. Strip those layers away and you have data about eyelids. On this view social science is closer to reading a text than to discovering laws.

## Why I Keep It

It sets the standard for the ethnographic side of my work. Code-switching is not mere alternation between languages; it is symbolic action: identity work, solidarity, status negotiation. Counting switches without interpreting them is thin description of something thick.

## Tensions

- Whose interpretation wins when researcher and participants disagree, or when meanings are contested within the community itself?
- Generalization has to be theoretical rather than statistical, and it is not always clear when that is enough.
