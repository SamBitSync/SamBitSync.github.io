# Methodological Holism

## The Idea

Social wholes have properties and causal powers that cannot be read off their individual members, so social phenomena have to be studied at their own level. Language shapes thought, but no individual invented it. Institutions constrain behavior and persist while their members turn over completely. Knowing every player's skill does not give you the team's dynamics.

The argument runs through emergence and top-down causation. Macro patterns like traffic jams arise from micro decisions but need their own concepts to describe; stronger cases, like social norms, constrain the very individuals who collectively sustain them. Against the individualist, the holist adds the regression problem: individual beliefs and preferences are themselves socially formed, so a purely individual starting point was never available.

In practice the live position is a middle one. Structures are real, but a structural explanation without any micro-level mechanism is description, not explanation. The working question is not "holism or individualism" but which level a given question needs.

## Why I Keep It

It flags when individual-level analysis will not suffice. Language vitality is not the sum of individual speakers: it is institutional support, domain allocation, and status hierarchies. Academic fields have emergent structures (paradigms, gatekeeping) that constrain individual researchers whatever they personally believe. It is one pole of a pair with methodological individualism; I use them as opposed lenses rather than picking a side.

## Tensions

- Without micro mechanisms, structural explanation risks being a redescription of the outcome.
- If structures determine behavior, where does agency sit, and how do structures ever change?
