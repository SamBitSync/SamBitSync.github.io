How does a pattern arise from the processes that sustain it? I keep returning to this question across language, artificial systems, and scientific practice. The materials change, and so does the kind of explanation that seems useful.

This map holds four ways of asking together: what inference is being performed, how a process unfolds through time, what mechanism produces it, and what it means in its setting.

## How I use the map

The orientations give me different questions to put to the same material. A Bayesian account asks about inference under uncertainty. A dynamical account follows trajectories, stability, and change. A mechanistic account asks which parts and interactions produce the pattern. A semiotic account attends to signs, interpretation, and the practices in which something acquires meaning.

The connections in the diagram are prompts for comparison. They do not establish that the approaches are interchangeable, or that a successful explanation in one vocabulary automatically translates into another. That translation is part of the work.

## Where it remains incomplete

An evolutionary question asks why this mechanism exists rather than another. An ecological question asks where the system's boundaries should be drawn. Both can change the problem before any of the four orientations begins to answer it.

I keep the map provisional so these questions can alter its structure. It is a record of an orientation that can change as the work develops.
